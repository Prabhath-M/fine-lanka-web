# Destinations Navigation Canvas — Design Direction

## Three stylistic approaches

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temple Observatory | A slate-and-brass celestial navigation table framed by stone-carved ritual geometry. It treats destination choice as a slow, ceremonial reading of the island. | 0.04 |
| Coastal Batik Passage | A lighter, textile-led route map where batik currents and indigo washes hold the cards like keepsakes. The feeling is warm and handcrafted rather than instrumental. | 0.07 |
| Sigiriya Cloud Court | A dark teal heritage atlas with a quiet cloud threshold around its central chart, drawing from Sigiriya fresco colour, Kandyan brass, and ancient stone reliefs. The map remains the spatial anchor rather than becoming a decorative wallpaper. | 0.03 |

## Chosen Approach: Sigiriya Cloud Court

**Design Movement.** The composition follows a contemporary interpretation of Sri Lankan palace cartography: an archival navigation table shaped by the mural pigments, cloud banks, and decorative cadence of the Sigiriya frescoes rather than a literal historical reconstruction.

**Core Principles.** The central map acts as one calm, contained field behind the cards. Heritage detail appears as an architectural frame, a brass navigation axis, and controlled botanical-spiral ornament instead of many competing textures. The rudder keeps its role as the operational centre of the section, and all peripheral controls recede until needed.

**Color Philosophy.** Deep temple teal creates visual depth and protects media contrast. Aged brass supplies hierarchy for interaction and coordinates, while faded fresco sand and cloud-ivory appear only at transitional edges. This avoids a white cloud box interrupting the atlas while retaining a soft atmospheric reveal.

**Layout Paradigm.** The section is arranged as a three-part ceremonial table: a narrow heritage header strip at the top, a central chart well with the cards suspended at its centre, and a low bearing ledger. Region filters live as two vertical compass registers at the outer edges, leaving the centre deliberately clear for navigation.

**Signature Elements.** A thin brass mandala ring sits behind the active card without obscuring the media. A cloud threshold is created through low-opacity, feathered edge veils—not a rectangular cloud image. Small Sigiriya-style liyawel tracery and lotus medallions appear only at the four chart corners.

**Interaction Philosophy.** Turning the rudder remains the primary physical act. Side filters behave like map registers with a restrained pressed state, while card movement and bearing updates continue to use the existing carousel logic.

**Animation.** The cards and rudder retain their current interaction timing. Background ornaments should only breathe through low-amplitude opacity changes; mist must never drift over card copy or control hit areas. All non-essential movement is suppressed for reduced-motion preferences.

**Typography System.** The existing editorial serif headline remains the ceremonial voice. The existing mono coordinate style becomes the cartographic utility voice, used sparingly for region labels, counts, bearings, and navigational marks.

**Brand Essence.** A heritage-informed Sri Lankan route atlas for travellers who want a journey shaped by place rather than a catalogue of stops. **Measured, luminous, rooted.**

**Brand Voice.** Headlines should sound like an invitation to orient oneself, while controls remain precise and nautical. Example lines: “Find a bearing through the island.” and “Turn toward the place that stays with you.”

**Wordmark & Logo.** Existing Fine Lanka branding remains untouched. Within this section, a small brass lotus-compass mark functions as the local navigational signature.

**Signature Brand Color.** **Sigiriya Deep Teal** — a dense blue-green that feels mineral, nocturnal, and distinctly suited to brass and fresco-sand accents.
